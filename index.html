Before we turn on your camera

PrepPulse can watch how your body responds while you practise, compared with your own calm baseline. Here is exactly what that means:

  • What we measure: your pulse, your breathing, and heart-rate variability (the small changes in time between heartbeats). The software reads these from tiny colour changes in your skin and small movements of your chest in the camera image.
  • Your video stays here: it is processed on this computer, never leaves it, and is never saved.
  • One check online: when a session starts, the measurement software (from a company called Presage) contacts Presage's servers to check this app's licence key and download its measurement models. No video or results are sent. Its usage reporting (telemetry) is turned off.
  • Your results stay here too: they are saved only on this computer, and you can delete them any time in Settings.
  • Not a medical tool: PrepPulse does not diagnose anything or give medical advice. It only shows how your readings change compared with your own baseline.

    What we noticed

      What do these numbers mean?

      Change from your calm baseline. Before the test, PrepPulse learned your own calm pulse and breathing. A value like +11 means your pulse was 11 beats per minute above that calm level at that moment. It is only compared with you, never with anyone else.

      Why some parts were not measured. The camera needs to see your face and upper chest. When you lean in or move out of view, measurement pauses. Right after a pause the pulse reading needs a few seconds to settle again. Those moments are shown as gaps; nothing is filled in or guessed.

      A pulse value covers the last few seconds. Each reading is worked out from roughly the previous 12 seconds, so the start of a question can still reflect the end of the one before.

      This is a camera estimate, not a medical measurement. PrepPulse is not a medical device and does not diagnose anything.

      It cannot tell why your body responded. A rise can come from many things: a hard question, the clock, how you are sitting, or nothing in particular. The map shows when it happened, not why.
